Variable Gage R&R (crossed)

Each operator measures each part several times, in random order, without seeing earlier readings.

  • IATF Every type of measurement system in the control plan needs a statistical study, with methods and acceptance criteria from the MSA reference manuals unless the customer approves others. IATF 16949, 7.1.5.1.1
  • AIAG The MSA manual's worked example uses 10 parts, 3 appraisers and 3 trials, the design most customers expect. Parts should come from the process and cover its whole operating range.
  • AIAG %GRR under 10% acceptable; 10% to 30% may be acceptable depending on the application; over 30% not acceptable. ndc of 5 or more. Gage resolution of one-tenth of the process variation or tolerance or finer.
  • ISO / VDA VDA Volume 5 (3rd ed., 2021) and ISO 22514-7:2021 judge measurement processes by measurement uncertainty (QMS, QMP) instead of %GRR. If your customer works to VDA 5, ask which studies they want; Mensuro doesn't calculate these yet.
  • Practice Pick parts by measuring a batch first and choosing ones spread across it. Ten parts bunched in the middle make ndc and %study variation look worse than the gage really is.
Customer example: Ford (Specifics for PPAP, Jan 2025)

One OEM's published minimums, for comparison. Other customers set their own; check yours.

  • At a minimum 10 parts, 3 operators, 3 trials, using the ANOVA method. Parts must represent a production run with all known sources of variation and use the full range of part-to-part variation. §2.2.8
  • Report %GRR as both % of study variation and % of tolerance. Under 10% of tolerance acceptable; 10% to 30% needs the site STA engineer; over 30% unacceptable. Hold parts in production-intent fixtures. §2.2.8

Attribute gage (go/no-go, visual)

Two common designs. The analytic method needs far fewer bad parts, but only parts close to the limit and a variable gage to measure them.

  • AIAG The MSA manual's attribute example uses 50 parts, 3 appraisers and 3 trials. Kappa above 0.75 is read as good to excellent agreement, below 0.40 as poor. For each appraiser, effectiveness of 90% or more, a miss rate of 2% or less and a false alarm rate of 5% or less are acceptable.
  • Practice Include parts close to both limits, on both sides of each, plus some clearly good and a few clearly bad. Measure every part with a variable gage first, so each has a known reference answer.
  • Practice The out-of-spec parts usually come from setup scrap or quarantined stock, measured and labeled first. Code the parts so appraisers can't recognise them, and randomise the order each trial.
Customer example: Ford (Specifics for PPAP, Jan 2025)

One OEM's published minimums, for comparison. Other customers set their own; check yours.

  • At a minimum 50 parts, 3 operators, 3 trials. Part mix: 25% near the lower limit and 25% near the upper limit (on both sides of each), 30% across the expected process variation, and 10% outside each gauge limit. Measure every part independently with a variable gage first. §2.2.8
  • All kappa values (between appraisers, within each appraiser, and each appraiser against the standard) should be greater than 0.75, and the gauge must reject every part outside the specification. §2.2.8
Limits to study
  • AIAG At least 8 parts with known reference values at roughly equal steps across the region around the limit, each checked 20 times. The part furthest outside is accepted 0 times and the part furthest inside 20 times; the 6 or more parts between are accepted 1 to 19 times. Each limit is studied separately. MSA 4th ed., as summarised by Minitab
  • Practice This answers "can I avoid making bad parts?" partly: you still need a part or two just beyond the limit, but not gross rejects, and far fewer than a 50-part agreement study.
  • Practice If the feature can be measured with a variable gage at all, a variable Gage R&R needs no bad parts and tells you more.
  • Practice Check with your customer first: some name the 50 × 3 × 3 agreement study as their minimum.

Attribute studies aren't in Mensuro yet. The variable Gage R&R and Type 1 studies are.

Type 1 gage study

One operator measures one reference part (with a calibrated value) repeatedly. It checks the gage's bias and repeatability before a Gage R&R.

  • Bosch 50 repeated readings of one reference part (at least 25), by one operator, in a short time.
  • Bosch Cg and Cgk of 1.33 or more, using 20% of the tolerance (Cg = 0.2T / 6s, Cgk = (0.1T − |bias|) / 3s). The bias is tested with a t-test. Resolution should be 5% of the tolerance or finer.
  • ISO / VDA VDA Volume 5 and ISO 22514-7 also start from repeated readings of a reference standard, but judge them through measurement uncertainty rather than Cg and Cgk.
  • Practice Choose a reference part close to the middle of the tolerance and use its calibration certificate value as the reference. Load and unload it between readings, as in normal use.

Initial process capability

Run the measurement system study first: capability numbers from an unproven gage can't be trusted.

  • AIAG PPAP acceptance for initial process studies: index above 1.67 acceptable; 1.33 to 1.67 may be acceptable with customer approval; below 1.33 not acceptable. The process should be stable (in statistical control) first.
  • ISO / VDA ISO 22514-2 defines Cp, Cpk, Pp and Ppk and the process models behind them.
  • Practice Collect 25 or more subgroups, commonly of 5 consecutive parts, spread over the run (control chart texts such as Montgomery base limits on 20 to 25 subgroups).
  • Practice For a multi-cavity tool, judge each cavity on its own: one cavity running off-center can hide inside a combined number that looks fine. Plan enough readings per cavity to judge it, and record the cavity number with every reading.
  • Practice If a cavity sits off-center but tool wear can only move it away from the near limit, write that up as an engineering justification on the report rather than leaving the reviewer to guess.
Customer example: Ford (Specifics for PPAP, Jan 2025)

One OEM's published minimums, for comparison. Other customers set their own; check yours.

  • At least 25 subgroups of at least 5 consecutive parts, for a minimum of 125 measurements from a run of at least 300 parts, spaced evenly through it. Complete the MSA first. §2.2.11.1
  • The data must include the full range of expected variation: all tools, all cavities, all streams, all shifts. Parts are measured from every cavity. Ppk of 1.67 or more, from a statistically stable data set. §2.2.9, §2.2.11.1

Reusing a Gage R&R on another part

Common, and often fine. The trouble starts when the other part's tolerance is tighter, or the gage was set up differently.

  • IATF Measurement systems are studied as they are used in the control plan. Whether a study carries over to another part is the customer's call, so give the reviewer the evidence. IATF 16949, 7.1.5.1.1
  • Practice Recheck the verdict at the new tolerance. Only %GRR of tolerance changes: halve the tolerance and the same gage error takes up twice as much of it.
  • Practice %GRR of study variation and ndc describe the parts in the original study. They only carry over if the new part's process varies about as much.

Before accepting a reused study, confirm

  1. Same gage, fixture and measurement method as the original study.
  2. Same type of feature, of a similar size, as the original part.
  3. The study's parts cover the range the new part's process is expected to produce.
  4. Same measurement location and conditions (temperature, operators, training).
  5. The gage hasn't been repaired, modified or re-programmed since the study.

Any "no" usually means a new study.

In Mensuro: run the original study in Gage R&R, then use "Using this study for another part?" on the results to recheck it at the new tolerance and print a reuse record.
