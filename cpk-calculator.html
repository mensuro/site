    Cp and Cpk: short-term capability

    Cp and Cpk use within (short-term) sigma: the variation from one part to the next, without slow drift or shifts.

    σwithin = MR̄ / 1.128  (one at a time)
    σwithin = R̄ / d2  (in groups; d2 depends on group size)

    Cp = (USL − LSL) / 6σwithin
    Cpk = min(USL − x̄, x̄ − LSL) / 3σwithin

    MR̄ is the average difference between consecutive measurements; R̄ is the average range within each group.

    Pp and Ppk: long-term capability

    Pp and Ppk use the standard deviation of every measurement, so they include drift and shifts over the whole study.

    s = √[Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)]

    Pp = (USL − LSL) / 6s
    Ppk = min(USL − x̄, x̄ − LSL) / 3s

    Cpk or Ppk: which does my customer want?

    Same formula, different sigma. Ppk is usually the one asked for in an initial PPAP study; Cpk shows what the process can do short-term. If Ppk is much lower than Cpk, the process is drifting or shifting over time.

    Cp vs Cpk: why centering matters

    Cp compares the tolerance width with the process spread and ignores where the process sits. Cpk measures from the mean to the nearer limit. A high Cp with a low Cpk means the process is capable but off-center, often an easy fix.

    What's a good Cpk? (the verdict)

    The verdict uses PPAP-style criteria on Ppk by default:

    • Above 1.67 acceptable
    • 1.33 to 1.67 may be acceptable with customer approval
    • Below 1.33 not acceptable

    Many automotive customers require 1.33 or more for ongoing production and 1.67 or more for initial studies. Your customer's limits apply if they differ.

    Expected and observed PPM

    Expected PPM is the share of a normal distribution beyond each limit, in parts per million, shown for both sigmas:

    PPM = [Φ((LSL − x̄) / σ) + 1 − Φ((USL − x̄) / σ)] × 1,000,000

    Observed PPM counts the measurements actually outside the limits.

    Is my data normal? (Anderson-Darling)

    With 8 or more measurements, an Anderson-Darling test checks whether a normal curve fits, using the D'Agostino-Stephens small-sample adjustment. If p is below 0.05, the data may not be normal, and the indices and expected PPM should be read with caution.

    One at a time or in groups?

    Choose one at a time if each measurement is a separate part taken in sequence. Choose in small groups if you pulled several parts together at each check, for example 5 parts every hour, and enter the measurements group by group. This only changes the short-term sigma behind Cp and Cpk; Pp and Ppk are the same either way.

    What if I only have one spec limit?

    Leave the other limit blank. You get the one-sided index (Ppu or Ppl, Cpu or Cpl); Cp and Pp need both limits and aren't shown.

    What has to be true for the results to hold?

    The process should be stable (in statistical control), and the measurements in the order they were taken, because the short-term sigma depends on order. Use 30 or more measurements for a dependable estimate; with fewer, the result carries a warning.

    Is my data stored anywhere?

    No. The calculation runs in your browser. The measurements and limits you enter are never sent to Mensuro or anyone else, and this page doesn't save them.
